      General starting points for introducing solids \u2014 every baby moves through these at their own pace, so treat this as a rough map, not a schedule.

      Around 6 months

      Start with thin purees or very soft mashes, offered once or twice a day, after milk (breast or formula) rather than instead of it. Don't expect much to actually get eaten at first — this stage is mostly about exploring taste and texture. Iron-rich foods matter here: a baby's iron stores from birth start dropping around this age, so fortified cereal, pureed meat, lentils, and beans are worth prioritizing early on.

      7–9 months

      Move toward thicker mashed or minced textures with some lumps, and start offering soft finger foods for self-feeding — steamed vegetable sticks, ripe soft fruit, strips of toast. This is also a good window to introduce an open or straw cup with water at meals. Most babies are up to 2–3 solid meals a day by the end of this stretch, alongside milk feeds.

      9–12 months

      Textures can move toward chopped, bite-sized table food, with more finger foods and some early practice with a spoon. Three meals plus a snack or two is common. Milk is often still the bulk of nutrition, but solids are playing a bigger role by now.

      Good habits at every stage

      • Introduce one new food at a time, and give it a couple of days before the next new one, so a reaction is easy to trace back.
      • Skip honey and unpasteurized foods before 12 months.
      • Watch for choking hazards — whole nuts, whole grapes, popcorn, and hard raw chunks are easier to manage cut small, sliced thin, or cooked soft.
      • None of this is a strict rulebook. If something here doesn't match what Jaxon's pediatrician has recommended for him specifically, follow their guidance instead.
